The machinery

Two passes, fused by rank — never by score

Meaning

Every note, paper and source file becomes a 1024-dimension vector via bge-m3, running locally. Your question lands in the same space; nearest neighbours win. Long documents are split, and scored by their best passage — so one relevant section surfaces a 40-page analysis note.

Catches paraphrase. Drifts on rare exact terms.

Keyword

A grep pass over the query's content words, normalised by √(document size) so a long file that says "list" nineteen times can't bury the note whose title is your query.

Nails exact terms. Blind to meaning.

Reciprocal Rank Fusion

score = 160 + rankmeaning + 160 + rankkeyword

A cosine of 0.57 and a match count of 19 are not comparable quantities — averaging them is meaningless. RRF combines the two orderings instead, which is why it needs no calibration between them.

The time dimension

A newer result should win. Not every older note should lose.

Results decay with age so an updated limit outranks the one it replaced. Tuned, not guessed: half-life and floor were swept against a labelled query set. Drag the controls — the curve is live.

Aggressive decay backfired. A 120-day half-life cost 0.16 MRR by pushing correct-but-older notes down. The plateau at 240 days / floor 0.50 is what ships.

Age can't tell a stale number from a still-valid method

A June limit is obsolete. A June derivation is not. So replacement is stated outright rather than inferred — and a measured result: marking one note superseded moved it from rank 2 to rank 19, while recency tuning alone moved it nowhere at any setting.
